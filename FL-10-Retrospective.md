# A Letter to My Week 1 Self: My FlyRank AI Internship Retrospective

Dear Week 1 Me,

When I started the FlyRank AI Internship, I had a simple but important
goal: I wanted to build an app that would make customer-service jobs
easier. I was particularly interested in reducing repetitive work and
slow response times. I knew that AI and automation could help, but I did
not yet fully understand what it would take to turn an idea into a
useful and reliable solution.

As the internship progressed, my understanding of AI development
changed. I learned that building a useful AI solution involves more than
giving instructions to a tool and accepting whatever it produces. It
requires identifying a real problem, defining what the solution should
do, writing clear prompts, testing outputs, identifying weaknesses and
making improvements.

One of my early learning experiences was the Week 2 Prompt Ladder
assignment. I started with a general customer-service prompt and
gradually improved it by adding context, tone, format and specific
instructions. I discovered that clearer and more relevant instructions
produce more useful responses. I also learned that adding unnecessary
examples can sometimes make an AI response less accurate. This taught me
to be intentional about how I communicate with AI.

During Week 3, I worked on presenting my portfolio and creating a visual
demonstration of a customer-support experience. I wanted to use a real
workplace inbox screenshot, but permission was not granted. Rather than
compromise workplace confidentiality, I created a fictional
customer-support inbox illustration using invented customer details.
This reinforced the importance of protecting information and being
honest about what a demonstration represents.

The project I am most proud of is my Week 8 Customer Email Triage and
Reply Assistant. It was exciting to see the result of collaborative work
with AI. I defined the problem, identified the intended users, developed
five email categories, established the customer-service tone and created
rules for escalating sensitive enquiries. I then configured and refined
the Claude Project instructions, tested the assistant with eight
synthetic email scenarios and reviewed the results.

The testing process showed me why human supervision matters. Some
initial outputs included unnecessary requests for personal information,
unsupported promises or internal notes in customer-facing drafts. I
identified these weaknesses and refined the instructions. By the final
evaluation, all eight scenarios reached their expected behaviour after
iterative improvements. This was a small qualitative test, not proof of
production-level reliability, but it showed me the value of testing and
refinement.

I also learned to recognise the limitations of what I had built. The
assistant requires emails to be copied into Claude manually. It cannot
connect to a live inbox, retrieve order information, verify company
policies or send emails automatically. A human must check, edit and
approve every response. Understanding these limitations is just as
important as demonstrating what the assistant can do.

Looking ahead, I want to develop this assistant into a functional
application that connects to an authorised customer-service inbox. I
would like it to classify incoming enquiries, prepare appropriate draft
replies, identify sensitive cases that require human attention and
reduce repetitive work. I want to build on what I have learned while
maintaining human oversight, privacy and accuracy.

There are three lessons I want to carry into every future project:

1.  **Clear prompts produce better results.** AI needs relevant context,
    specific instructions and clear expectations.
2.  **Human supervision is essential.** AI can assist with tasks, but
    people must review important decisions and verify information before
    acting.
3.  **AI should be used effectively, efficiently, ethically and
    safely.** Saving time should never come at the expense of accuracy,
    privacy, responsibility or people's trust.

To my Week 1 self, I would say: keep learning, stay curious and do not
be discouraged when the first result is not perfect. The process of
testing, identifying problems and improving your work is part of
building something valuable.

I started this internship wanting to build an app that would make
customer-service work easier. I am ending it with a clearer
understanding of how to approach that goal, practical experience
collaborating with AI and a stronger sense of responsibility as I
continue learning about AI automation.

This is not the end of the journey. It is the foundation for what I want
to build next.

**Genevieve Jacob**
